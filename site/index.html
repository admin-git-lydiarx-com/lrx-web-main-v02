Who we are

Your genome should not have to emigrate to be read.

78%of participants in genome-wide association studies worldwide are of European ancestry (Sirugo, Williams and Tishkoff, Cell, 2019).
1anonymous donor supplied most of the sequence of the human reference genome against which, for two decades, everyone else has been read.

Most of the world's genomic reference data describes a minority of the world's people. The countries missing from it are told, in effect, to send their samples abroad and wait. LydiaRx makes the opposite possible. A national cohort is sequenced in-country, held on infrastructure the State controls and governed to a published standard. It is then opened to the world's researchers on the country's terms. The data never moves. The revenue is shared.

We are not a sequencing vendor, a data broker or a consultancy. We are a delivery partner. We finance the first step privately, operate the secure environment, bring the research partners and hand the country a working national asset. In every country we form a local presence (a locally registered company or office, national staff and a director resident for the pilot). We are a counterpart under the country's own law, not a visitor. Our people built the programmes that others cite.

Questions governments ask
  • Who owns the data?

    The State. A public institution holds it under the Charter. A national-majority committee decides every use. LydiaRx takes no ownership of samples or data, during the pilot or after it.

  • What happens if LydiaRx disappears?

    Nothing we build depends on us staying. The laboratory, equipment and data environment are installed in the institution and offered to it; the data never left; every governance document is published; the hosting agreement provides for continuity of the platform.

  • Can researchers abroad use the data?

    Yes, by bringing their question to it. They receive governed, non-identifying results with a provenance certificate. Never a copy. The committee can refuse any request. The ledger records every one.
